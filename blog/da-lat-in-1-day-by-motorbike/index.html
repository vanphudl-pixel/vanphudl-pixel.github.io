Da Lat in 1 Day by Motorbike: A Full Loop from Sunrise to the Night Market

Only have one day in Da Lat? With your own scooter you can see the lake, a mosaic pagoda, a waterfall, a pine-forest lake and the night market — without rushing. Here's a tried-and-tested loop of about 40–50 km.

Updated: Oct 28, 2026

Is one day in Da Lat enough?

One day won't show you everything — the tea hills, Lang Biang and the big waterfalls further south need more time. But Da Lat is compact, and with a scooter one well-planned day covers the city's classic sights plus two of the nicest places just outside it. The trick is to start early, ride the outer parts in the dry morning and keep the afternoon flexible, because showers often come after lunch in the rainy season.

The one-day loop at a glance

  • Total distance: about 40–50 km, all on paved roads.
  • Riding time: roughly 2–2.5 hours in total; the rest is sightseeing, coffee and food.
  • Directions: east to Trai Mat in the morning, the centre around midday, south to Datanla and Tuyen Lam Lake in the afternoon, the market in the evening.

Early morning: Xuan Huong Lake (6:00–7:00)

Pick up your bike the evening before or when our shop opens at 4:30 am, then ride a slow loop around Xuan Huong Lake (about 5 km). In the early morning the air is cool, mist sometimes sits on the water and locals jog and exercise along the shore. Have a bowl of noodle soup or a bánh mì and a hot coffee before heading out of town.

Morning: Linh Phuoc Pagoda in Trai Mat (7:30–9:00)

Ride east about 8 km to Trai Mat village. Linh Phuoc Pagoda is famous for its decoration made of broken porcelain and glass, a tall bell tower and a long mosaic dragon. Dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered) and keep your voice down inside. On the way back, stop at Da Lat Railway Station, the 1930s Art Deco building with three pointed roofs — old carriages stand on the platform for photos.

Late morning: the pink church and lunch (10:00–12:30)

Domaine de Marie, the pink church on a small hill west of the market, is free to visit and lovely in the morning light — see our Domaine de Marie guide for hours and photo angles. Then have lunch around the market: bánh căn, bún bò or a simple rice dish are all easy to find. More ideas for the centre are in things to do in central Da Lat.

Afternoon: Datanla and Tuyen Lam Lake (13:30–16:30)

Ride south about 5–7 km on the road towards Prenn Pass to Datanla Waterfall, known for its alpine coaster through the forest. Continue a few kilometres to Tuyen Lam Lake, surrounded by pine hills, and Truc Lam Monastery above it. You can also reach the monastery by the Robin Hill cable car. Our Tuyen Lam Lake and Truc Lam guide has the route and tips. If a shower comes, wait it out in a café by the lake — it usually passes quickly.

Sunset and evening: back to the centre

Head back before dark. Catch the last light from a hillside café, then park in a guarded lot and walk to the night market for grilled snacks, hot soy milk and street food. Our night market guide tells you what to try.

If you have more time

With a second day, add the Cau Dat tea hills at sunrise or Lang Biang — see Da Lat in 2 days or Da Lat in 3 days by motorbike, and our motorbike routes around Da Lat.

Tips for the day

  • Bring a light jacket and keep a raincoat under the seat.
  • Park only in guarded lots (gửi xe) and keep the ticket.
  • Save the route in Google Maps offline before you leave.
  • Ride on the right, slow down before bends, no alcohol when riding — and strictly follow Vietnamese traffic law.

Which bike for a one-day loop

A Honda Vision 110cc is perfect for this loop alone; with a passenger the Honda Air Blade 125cc feels more relaxed on the hills towards Datanla. An electric scooter (2 batteries, about 50–60 km in total) can do the loop, but you will want to ride calmly.
